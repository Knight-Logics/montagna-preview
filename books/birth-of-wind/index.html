THE STORY

In a world that denied her a place, she became a legend.

A WORK OF FICTION

In Japan in 1333, a female samurai fights for her place in a society dominated by men. Her discipline, determination, and skill carry her through a life of conflict. When her story reaches its end, a curse placed on her sword by the shogun binds the warrior and the blade together for eternity. Birth of Wind begins a trilogy that carries her legacy across centuries.

Read the full published book description ＋

A female samurai warrior in 1333 is placed in the middle of the most male-dominated era in history. Her determination, skills, and disciplined fighting succeeded to the end, and her demise along with a curse placed upon her sword by the Shogun in which she and the sword would be entwined for eternity.

A reader’s guide to Birth of Wind

The story, the editions, and the reading order for Birth of Wind are on this page. The guide below is here so the novel can be found by the searches that actually describe it, and so the next book is a link rather than a dead end.

Birth of Wind opens the Wind Trilogy in Japan in 1333. The published description sets a female samurai against a society that will not make a place for her, and it ends with a curse the shogun lays on her sword, binding the warrior and the blade. That is the spine of the novel on this site. The page does not pretend to replace the book, and it does not add battles, names, or a last chapter the published description does not support.

Readers looking for historical fantasy keep meeting the phrase dual timeline. A market snapshot of that lane dated 26 October 2026 named the dual timeline as the signal then rising. This trilogy is built that way on purpose: the first novel is the fourteenth century, and the later novels carry the same sword into 1996 and then across the Pacific. Calling the book a European medieval romance would be the wrong search. The hero’s journey is a fair description of the shape, which is the phrase rising in the broader medieval lane of that same snapshot, but the world on the page is Japan.

Fantasy was also the genre both most written and most requested on QueryTracker as of 17 December 2025. That demand is crowded. Birth of Wind is not part of the romantasy boom, and this page will not borrow that label. It is historical fantasy with a military life at the center. If that is the shelf you came for, start here, then go forward. Do not start with the modern novels and hope the curse will explain itself.

Five further novels sit one link away from this page. Each note below is the published situation of that book, not a new plot. Follow the one that matches the search you actually typed.

The Paradise of the Devil begins with a Cuban colonel who has lost faith in the condition of the military. He confronts Castro, kills him, and takes command of the island, expecting the people to support the takeover. A civilian army rises against his troops instead. Under that civil war sits a secret left from the Cuban Missile Crisis, and the secret pulls the United States into a confrontation that does not stay in Cuba. Continue on The Paradise of the Devil.

The Warning of Ramses starts from Egypt’s dependence on the Nile. Ethiopia builds a dam on the Blue Nile and sharply reduces the water reaching Egypt. The dispute moves toward war. Libya joins Egypt. The confrontation also involves the United States and the dam project. A river becomes a regional crisis. That is the published spine, and it is enough to tell you the novel is about water, alliances, and the point where a resource war stops being a negotiation. Continue on The Warning of Ramses.

Operation Glass Slipper begins with a scientific gift. A doctor finds a microbe that lives by consuming carbon in crude oil. With a microbiologist, he alters it so the organism can travel through the air and consume carbon pollution. The same discovery is then seen by a small group of terrorists as a path toward a biological weapon. The published description holds both uses in one research project: a repair for the atmosphere, and a weapon made from that repair. Continue on Operation Glass Slipper.

Operation Dragon Claw puts a terrorist group on La Palma. They intend to place explosives along a crack in the Cumbre Vieja volcano. In the novel’s scenario, the collapse would throw an enormous mass of earth into the Atlantic and raise a tsunami. Dragon Fire troops are sent to stop the attack before the consequences leave the island. The published description is careful to call that collapse the novel’s scenario. This preview repeats that caution. It is fiction about a plan, not a forecast. Continue on Operation Dragon Claw.

Trump’s Brass Escalator continues Sandstorm. The president, the vice-president, Special Forces leader Walker, and his female sergeant are in New York to present evidence gathered in Iran to the United Nations. Before they can, robbers attack Trump Tower and take the vice-president hostage. Walker’s team has to break off the larger mission to stop the robbery and save a life at the center of government. Continue on Trump’s Brass Escalator.
