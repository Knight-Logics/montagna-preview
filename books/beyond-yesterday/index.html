THE STORY

To save the future, they must steal lives from the past.

A WORK OF FICTION

After a nuclear exchange destroys most of the world’s population, survivors develop a way to reach into the past. Their first attempts to replenish humanity using reproductive material fail. They turn instead to taking people from earlier times, including the pilots of five aircraft that disappeared in the Bermuda Triangle. Each journey leaves consequences in the Atlantic, linking the mysteries of the past to a desperate effort to preserve the human future.

Read the full published book description ＋

After a nuclear exchange destroys over 85% of the population of the world, the survivors developed the means to go back in time and they start kidnapping people from the past to repopulate the earth as it cools down and becomes survivable again. First, they took eggs from women and sperm from males but they would not survive so they took to kidnapping souls. They took the pilots of the five aircraft that went missing in the triangle and each time they came to get more souls from the past, their entry caused a Hurricane in the Atlantic, but it was the only way to keep the human alive in the future.

IN THE LIBRARY

A reader’s guide to Beyond Yesterday

The story, the editions, and the reading order for Beyond Yesterday are on this page. The guide below is here so the novel can be found by the searches that actually describe it, and so the next book is a link rather than a dead end.

Beyond Yesterday begins after a nuclear exchange has destroyed most of the world’s population. Survivors learn how to reach into the past. Attempts to replenish humanity with reproductive material fail. They turn to taking people from earlier times, including the pilots of five aircraft that disappeared in the Bermuda Triangle. Each journey leaves consequences in the Atlantic. The mysteries of those disappearances become part of a desperate effort to keep a human future.

Speculative fiction and science fiction are the honest shelves, even though science fiction is a genre agents were requesting less often than fantasy by December 2025. This preview will not relabel the novel as fantasy to catch that demand. Dual timeline, the rising historical-fantasy phrase, is a distant cousin: the book does move between a ruined future and earlier days. It is not a samurai history. That story is the Wind Trilogy.

Readers who arrived from mystery-thriller searches will find disappearances and a conspiracy of survival, not a detective case. Operation Glass Slipper is the other novel in which a scientific project meant to save people becomes a danger. Beyond Yesterday is the one in which the saving has already failed and the past is the remaining material.

Beyond Yesterday belongs with the speculative and biological novels, not with the domestic thrillers whose search interest climbed through early 2026. Mystery thriller books, as a phrase, drew more Google search interest than sci-fi novels or “fantasy fiction trends” in a comparison covering June 2025 through June 2026, and peaked in October 2025. Some readers will arrive through that door. The honest names for this novel are speculative fiction, a science-driven thriller, or a containment story, depending on the book. Science fiction was a less requested genre among agents by December 2025. That fact is not a reason to pretend Beyond Yesterday is historical fantasy. The dual-timeline Wind novels are a different series.

Five further novels sit one link away from this page. Each note below is the published situation of that book, not a new plot. Follow the one that matches the search you actually typed.

Night Wind closes the trilogy’s published arc on this site. The sword is discovered by the FBI. The agent who takes responsibility brings it to the United States and learns it is not an ordinary weapon. Wind remains bound to protect the person who possesses it. That duty, not a new origin story, is what the third novel puts in a new country. Continue on Night Wind.

The Vector is the preview’s plain example of a geopolitical thriller. Iran answers a war with a covert biological attack on the United States. Eight volunteers are fitted with false teeth that hold a deadly agent and are sent through Mexico toward eight American cities. Their orders are to release the agent and spread the infection before it kills them. The weapon in the published description is a person, not a missile. Continue on The Vector.

God’s Sword of Justice places terrorists in the control room of the Indian Point nuclear power plant in New York. They shut off cooling water to the reactor. The published description gives the region a nine-hour window before a catastrophic meltdown and the contamination that would follow. Walker’s elite soldiers have to stop the attackers before the plant itself becomes the weapon. Continue on God’s Sword of Justice.

Bio-Level Three sends specialized Dragon Fire troops into the Libyan desert. A rebel-led military command is secretly building twin chemical and biological factories. American intelligence finds the project. The president orders the factories destroyed before they can operate. The published description is a preemption story: stop the buildings before the weapons exist, not investigate a crime after a city is already sick. Continue on Bio-Level Three.

Code Cold Fire Alert is set in the plants the United States builds to destroy its own chemical and biological stockpiles. Crews are sealed inside for long shifts and ordered to stay if a spill cannot be contained. A spill happens. Doctors try to leave after disconnecting a nuclear sterilization device. A lone colonel goes into the containment area to restore its power, knowing he may have to trigger the final safeguard by hand. Continue on Code Cold Fire Alert.
