THE STORY

A discovery that could heal the atmosphere—or become a weapon.

A WORK OF FICTION

A doctor discovers a microbe that survives by consuming carbon in crude oil. Working with a microbiologist, he alters it so it can travel through the air and consume the carbon polluting the atmosphere. The discovery promises an extraordinary environmental benefit. But a small group of terrorists sees a different possibility and seeks to exploit the research in the development of a biological weapon.

IN THE LIBRARY

A reader’s guide to Operation Glass Slipper

The story, the editions, and the reading order for Operation Glass Slipper are on this page. The guide below is here so the novel can be found by the searches that actually describe it, and so the next book is a link rather than a dead end.

Operation Glass Slipper begins with a scientific gift. A doctor finds a microbe that lives by consuming carbon in crude oil. With a microbiologist, he alters it so the organism can travel through the air and consume carbon pollution. The same discovery is then seen by a small group of terrorists as a path toward a biological weapon. The published description holds both uses in one research project: a repair for the atmosphere, and a weapon made from that repair.

Readers searching speculative fiction, near-future thriller, or a science thriller will be closer than readers searching cozy mystery or romantasy. Mystery thriller remains a high-interest search, and this novel uses a thriller’s chase rather than a fairy tale, whatever the title suggests. The title is ironic. The microbe is not a ballgown. Antagonist motivation is a rising phrase in the medical-thriller lane of the late October 2026 snapshot. Here the motive is the theft of a beneficial organism. That is as far as the medical label should be pushed.

The Vector and Bio-Level Three are the other biological threats, one carried by people and one built in a desert factory. Glass Slipper is the story in which the danger begins as a cure.

Operation Glass Slipper belongs with the speculative and biological novels, not with the domestic thrillers whose search interest climbed through early 2026. Mystery thriller books, as a phrase, drew more Google search interest than sci-fi novels or “fantasy fiction trends” in a comparison covering June 2025 through June 2026, and peaked in October 2025. Some readers will arrive through that door. The honest names for this novel are speculative fiction, a science-driven thriller, or a containment story, depending on the book. Science fiction was a less requested genre among agents by December 2025. That fact is not a reason to pretend Operation Glass Slipper is historical fantasy. The dual-timeline Wind novels are a different series.

Five further novels sit one link away from this page. Each note below is the published situation of that book, not a new plot. Follow the one that matches the search you actually typed.

Operation Dragon Claw puts a terrorist group on La Palma. They intend to place explosives along a crack in the Cumbre Vieja volcano. In the novel’s scenario, the collapse would throw an enormous mass of earth into the Atlantic and raise a tsunami. Dragon Fire troops are sent to stop the attack before the consequences leave the island. The published description is careful to call that collapse the novel’s scenario. This preview repeats that caution. It is fiction about a plan, not a forecast. Continue on Operation Dragon Claw.

Trump’s Brass Escalator continues Sandstorm. The president, the vice-president, Special Forces leader Walker, and his female sergeant are in New York to present evidence gathered in Iran to the United Nations. Before they can, robbers attack Trump Tower and take the vice-president hostage. Walker’s team has to break off the larger mission to stop the robbery and save a life at the center of government. Continue on Trump’s Brass Escalator.

The Scorpion Sting begins after Eagle’s Talon. Saddam Hussein sends another group into the United States to kill the Iraqi operative American forces captured, and to kill soldiers involved in that arrest. Walker’s team has to find the new cell. The danger follows the soldiers home. The published description is a retaliation story, which is why the series page treats it as the continuation rather than as a second first chapter. Continue on The Scorpion Sting.

Birth of Wind opens the Wind Trilogy in Japan in 1333. The published description sets a female samurai against a society that will not make a place for her, and it ends with a curse the shogun lays on her sword, binding the warrior and the blade. That is the spine of the novel on this site. The page does not pretend to replace the book, and it does not add battles, names, or a last chapter the published description does not support. Continue on Birth of Wind.

The Paradise of the Devil begins with a Cuban colonel who has lost faith in the condition of the military. He confronts Castro, kills him, and takes command of the island, expecting the people to support the takeover. A civilian army rises against his troops instead. Under that civil war sits a secret left from the Cuban Missile Crisis, and the secret pulls the United States into a confrontation that does not stay in Cuba. Continue on The Paradise of the Devil.
