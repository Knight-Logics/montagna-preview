## A microbe that could clean the sky—or become a weapon

Operation Glass Slipper is a science thriller that begins with a hopeful discovery. A doctor finds a microbe living in crude oil that survives by devouring carbon particles. With a female microbiologist he alters it, making it lighter than air so it can chase down and eat the carbon polluting the air we breathe, and giving it a hard shell called a sheen.

That is the promise. The threat arrives when a small group of terrorists learns of the work and plans to use it to perfect a biological weapon of mass destruction. The novel sits where environmental science and bioterrorism meet: the same engineering that could heal the atmosphere is what makes the organism dangerous. It is on the [New Release shelf](https://stephenfmontagnabooks.com/new-release/) with [The Vector](https://stephenfmontagnabooks.com/books/the-vector/), the other new novel about a biological threat.

### Is Operation Glass Slipper climate fiction?

In part. Its starting point is an environmental fix—an engineered microbe that eats carbon in the air—but the story is a thriller about that discovery being turned toward a weapon. Readers of climate fiction and of bioterror thrillers will both find their subject in it.

### Is Operation Glass Slipper a standalone novel?

Yes. Operation Glass Slipper stands alone; no other book needs to be read first.
