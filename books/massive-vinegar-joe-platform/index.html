Read the full published book description ＋

China uses a ploy to attack North Korea with the blessing of the United Nations, with the promise of eliminating North Korea's nuclear weapons. China continues its deception in order to sell Hong Kong to the English with the true purpose of plundering its riches and attacking Taiwan. The United States, having no military bases in an Asian region, is forced to connect a massive floating base for the safety of the world.

IN THE LIBRARY

A reader’s guide to Massive Vinegar Joe Platform

The story, the editions, and the reading order for Massive Vinegar Joe Platform are on this page. The guide below is here so the novel can be found by the searches that actually describe it, and so the next book is a link rather than a dead end.

Massive Vinegar Joe Platform is a Pacific confrontation. China seeks United Nations approval for an attack on North Korea, presented as an effort to eliminate nuclear weapons. Behind that promise is a broader deception involving Hong Kong and Taiwan. In the novel’s scenario the United States has no nearby base, and it turns to a massive floating platform. The published description is about pretext, alliance, and a machine built because the map will not cooperate.

Geopolitical thriller, military thriller, and espionage are the searches that fit. Political intrigue fits the United Nations scene without requiring the ancient-world label attached to that rising phrase. High stakes fits a war proposed as disarmament. This is not a historical novel about the Second World War, even though wartime fiction remains a strong separate appetite. The platform and the pretext are the story this page is willing to claim.

The Warning of Ramses is the other novel in which a public crisis, a dam rather than a resolution, reorganizes a region. The nuclear novels elsewhere on the shelf are raids and countdowns. Vinegar Joe is the one about permission, deception, and a fleet that has to invent its own ground.

Five further novels sit one link away from this page. Each note below is the published situation of that book, not a new plot. Follow the one that matches the search you actually typed.

The Vector is the preview’s plain example of a geopolitical thriller. Iran answers a war with a covert biological attack on the United States. Eight volunteers are fitted with false teeth that hold a deadly agent and are sent through Mexico toward eight American cities. Their orders are to release the agent and spread the infection before it kills them. The weapon in the published description is a person, not a missile. Continue on The Vector.

God’s Sword of Justice places terrorists in the control room of the Indian Point nuclear power plant in New York. They shut off cooling water to the reactor. The published description gives the region a nine-hour window before a catastrophic meltdown and the contamination that would follow. Walker’s elite soldiers have to stop the attackers before the plant itself becomes the weapon. Continue on God’s Sword of Justice.

Bio-Level Three sends specialized Dragon Fire troops into the Libyan desert. A rebel-led military command is secretly building twin chemical and biological factories. American intelligence finds the project. The president orders the factories destroyed before they can operate. The published description is a preemption story: stop the buildings before the weapons exist, not investigate a crime after a city is already sick. Continue on Bio-Level Three.

Code Cold Fire Alert is set in the plants the United States builds to destroy its own chemical and biological stockpiles. Crews are sealed inside for long shifts and ordered to stay if a spill cannot be contained. A spill happens. Doctors try to leave after disconnecting a nuclear sterilization device. A lone colonel goes into the containment area to restore its power, knowing he may have to trigger the final safeguard by hand. Continue on Code Cold Fire Alert.

Eagle’s Talon is the opening novel of the Scorpion Trilogy. In the published story, Saddam Hussein sends a terrorist group to assassinate the American president in an effort to end sanctions. Robert Walker and his Special Forces troops are sent to stop the attack, and the mission then takes them into Iraq to capture the terrorist leader and bring him to the United States for trial. Continue on Eagle’s Talon.
