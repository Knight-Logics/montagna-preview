A WORK OF FICTION

Wind’s sword is discovered once again, this time by the FBI. The agent responsible for it brings the blade back to the United States and soon learns that it is far more than an ordinary weapon. Wind remains bound to protect the person who possesses it, bringing the warrior’s enduring obligation into the agent’s life. The third novel carries the Wind Trilogy across the Pacific into a new setting.

IN THE LIBRARY

A reader’s guide to Night Wind

The story, the editions, and the reading order for Night Wind are on this page. The guide below is here so the novel can be found by the searches that actually describe it, and so the next book is a link rather than a dead end.

Night Wind closes the trilogy’s published arc on this site. The sword is discovered by the FBI. The agent who takes responsibility brings it to the United States and learns it is not an ordinary weapon. Wind remains bound to protect the person who possesses it. That duty, not a new origin story, is what the third novel puts in a new country.

Search interest in fantasy stays high, and historical fantasy’s rising dual-timeline signal is really a trilogy-shaped idea: one life, more than one century, more than one country. Night Wind is the American chapter of that idea. Readers who typed military thriller and landed here have crossed shelves. The Scorpion novels are the military-thriller trilogy. This one is still Wind’s story, told after the sword has left Japan.

The honest reading order is Birth of Wind, then The Wind’s Breathe, then Night Wind. The series page says the same thing. Skipping ahead will still show you an agent and a sword. It will not show you why the protection is a curse rather than a choice.

Readers reach Night Wind with a handful of living searches. Historical fantasy is the shelf, and the dual timeline is the shape: fourteenth-century Japan, then 1996, then the United States. A 26 October 2026 snapshot of that lane named the dual timeline as the signal on the rise. Fantasy itself was the genre authors wrote most and agents requested most on QueryTracker as of 17 December 2025, which makes the shelf crowded and makes a false label expensive. This preview does not call the Wind novels romantasy, cozy fantasy, or a European medieval. The hero’s journey is a fair description of the arc and is the phrase then rising in the wider medieval lane, but the country is Japan. Start at Birth of Wind, continue with The Wind’s Breathe, and finish with Night Wind. The map is on the Wind series page.

Five further novels sit one link away from this page. Each note below is the published situation of that book, not a new plot. Follow the one that matches the search you actually typed.

Daughter of the Scorpion turns on secret military papers that expose an Iraqi biological weapons program. American intelligence learns the pathogen could mutate and threaten life on a global scale if it is released. Captain Robert Walker leads a rapid response team into the Iraqi desert to find a young woman believed to know where the weapon is hidden. The clock in the published description is that search, not a courtroom drama and not a laboratory procedural. Continue on Daughter of the Scorpion.

The Wind’s Breathe is the second novel. Centuries after Birth of Wind, the cursed sword is found in Japan in 1996. Its new owner turns Wind’s samurai abilities against rivals in the construction industry. The published description is that specific: not a return to the fourteenth-century war, but the same binding put to work in a modern commercial fight. The title on the author’s materials uses this spelling, and so does this preview. Continue on The Wind’s Breathe.

The Vulture Will Lead You to Death follows terrorists who enter the United States from Cuba with a plan against the Port of Miami. They know a supertanker is docked there. They do not know that a ship carrying military supplies is stationed nearby, and that its presence could magnify the blast. The published description stops on that unawareness. The novel is the working out of an attack whose second weapon is the port itself. Continue on The Vulture Will Lead You to Death.

IFF: Identify Friend or Foe turns on a stolen aircraft. An Iranian pilot takes an F-18 and turns it against an American carrier in the Persian Gulf. The friendly identification signal, the system built to stop friendly fire, also stops the fleet from treating the aircraft as an enemy. A safeguard becomes the opening for the attack. The title is the premise, written out so a reader can see the trap before the novel springs it. Continue on IFF: Identify Friend or Foe.

Operation Dragon Fire follows an Iraqi terrorist who was admitted to the United States after helping American troops. He recruits a group for coordinated attacks. Targets in New York and Washington are meant to conceal a harder objective: a nuclear strike between the White House and the Capitol. The Dragon Fire troops are called to stop a plan that depends on distraction, timing, and attacks that grow worse while the real target stays hidden. Continue on Operation Dragon Fire.

Those five are a path, not the whole library. All 21 novels remain listed together, the two trilogies stay in order on Series, and the twelve portfolios stay on Flipbooks.
