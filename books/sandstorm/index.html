THE STORY

A covert mission into Iran to stop a nuclear threat.

A WORK OF FICTION

A rogue Russian admiral sells Iran nuclear-tipped submarine-launched missiles. American special operations soldiers are sent into Iran to destroy the missiles and the submarines that carry them. They must also recover evidence of the transfer of weapons and technology—evidence that could expose how Iran acquired the means to build and launch its own nuclear threat against the United States.

IN THE LIBRARY

A reader’s guide to Sandstorm

The story, the editions, and the reading order for Sandstorm are on this page. The guide below is here so the novel can be found by the searches that actually describe it, and so the next book is a link rather than a dead end.

Sandstorm sends American special operations soldiers into Iran. A rogue Russian admiral has sold Iran nuclear-tipped missiles launched from submarines. The team has to destroy the missiles and the submarines, and it has to bring back evidence of the transfer, evidence that could show how Iran acquired the means to build and launch a nuclear threat against the United States. The published description is a raid plus a proof.

Military thriller, espionage, and high stakes are the matching searches. Spy thriller fits the evidence as much as the explosives. Corporate corruption is a rising military-thriller phrase in the October 2026 lane snapshot. A rogue admiral selling missiles is a betrayal inside a navy, which is as close as this novel comes. It is not a story about a company. Trump’s Brass Escalator is the continuation: the evidence from Iran is what the president and vice-president mean to carry to the United Nations, before Trump Tower is attacked.

Read Sandstorm before that continuation. The Scorpion Trilogy is Walker’s other war, and it should not be folded into this mission unless you are only browsing the same kind of novel.

Five further novels sit one link away from this page. Each note below is the published situation of that book, not a new plot. Follow the one that matches the search you actually typed.

Operation Dragon Fire follows an Iraqi terrorist who was admitted to the United States after helping American troops. He recruits a group for coordinated attacks. Targets in New York and Washington are meant to conceal a harder objective: a nuclear strike between the White House and the Capitol. The Dragon Fire troops are called to stop a plan that depends on distraction, timing, and attacks that grow worse while the real target stays hidden. Continue on Operation Dragon Fire.

Beyond Yesterday begins after a nuclear exchange has destroyed most of the world’s population. Survivors learn how to reach into the past. Attempts to replenish humanity with reproductive material fail. They turn to taking people from earlier times, including the pilots of five aircraft that disappeared in the Bermuda Triangle. Each journey leaves consequences in the Atlantic. The mysteries of those disappearances become part of a desperate effort to keep a human future. Continue on Beyond Yesterday.

Daughter of the Scorpion turns on secret military papers that expose an Iraqi biological weapons program. American intelligence learns the pathogen could mutate and threaten life on a global scale if it is released. Captain Robert Walker leads a rapid response team into the Iraqi desert to find a young woman believed to know where the weapon is hidden. The clock in the published description is that search, not a courtroom drama and not a laboratory procedural. Continue on Daughter of the Scorpion.

The Wind’s Breathe is the second novel. Centuries after Birth of Wind, the cursed sword is found in Japan in 1996. Its new owner turns Wind’s samurai abilities against rivals in the construction industry. The published description is that specific: not a return to the fourteenth-century war, but the same binding put to work in a modern commercial fight. The title on the author’s materials uses this spelling, and so does this preview. Continue on The Wind’s Breathe.

The Vulture Will Lead You to Death follows terrorists who enter the United States from Cuba with a plan against the Port of Miami. They know a supertanker is docked there. They do not know that a ship carrying military supplies is stationed nearby, and that its presence could magnify the blast. The published description stops on that unawareness. The novel is the working out of an attack whose second weapon is the port itself. Continue on The Vulture Will Lead You to Death.
