IN THE LIBRARY

What the 2026 shelf actually holds

2026 collection holds five novels: Operation Dragon Fire, Bio-Level Three, Operation Dragon Claw, Beyond Yesterday, and Code Cold Fire Alert. Dragon Fire hides a nuclear strike between the White House and the Capitol inside lesser attacks. Bio-Level Three is the desert order to destroy chemical and biological factories before they operate. Dragon Claw is the plan to crack the Cumbre Vieja volcano on La Palma. Cold Fire is the sealed American plant where a colonel may have to fire the last safeguard by hand. Beyond Yesterday is the novel after a nuclear exchange, when survivors reach into the past, including aircraft lost in the Bermuda Triangle.

Cold Fire is the newest flipbook, sixty pages on the novel’s own page. Bio-Level Three and Beyond Yesterday also have readers. Dragon Fire and Dragon Claw do not yet. Use flipbooks if you want the grid rather than the shelf.

These are not chapters of one operation. Dragon Fire, Bio-Level Three, and Dragon Claw share the Dragon Fire troops across three different targets. Cold Fire is a different command and a different building. Beyond Yesterday leaves the special-operations pattern entirely and becomes speculative fiction. The shelf is a year group from the author’s site.

The searches this library can honestly meet are historical fantasy and the dual timeline, for Wind Trilogy; and military thriller, spy thriller, espionage, high stakes, action thriller, ensemble cast, and series continuation, for Scorpion Trilogy and the standalone operations. “Mystery thriller books” drew more search interest than sci-fi novels or “fantasy fiction trends” from June 2025 through June 2026, with a peak in October 2025, which is why a thriller reader may arrive here and should then be shown the right shelf. Domestic thriller interest climbed into February 2026, and romantasy remains a separate boom. Neither phrase is a description of these novels, so the pages do not wear those labels. Fantasy was the most written and most requested genre on QueryTracker as of 17 December 2025. Birth of Wind is this library’s fantasy. It is not a invitation to relabel the Iran operations as fantasy because the larger genre is crowded.

Privacy, Terms of use, and Copyright are the three policy pages. They are written for this preview: a local cart that never charges a card, a reading list that stays in the browser, a contact form that does not send, YouTube trailers, and flipbook images hosted here. The author’s official site did not present those policies at its homepage or at /privacy/. These pages do not pretend to govern that site.

For the numbered teams, leave this shelf and open Scorpion Trilogy or Wind Trilogy. For the newest geopolitical cluster, open New Release.

Five further novels sit one link away from this page. Each note below is the published situation of that book, not a new plot. Follow the one that matches the search you actually typed.

IFF: Identify Friend or Foe turns on a stolen aircraft. An Iranian pilot takes an F-18 and turns it against an American carrier in the Persian Gulf. The friendly identification signal, the system built to stop friendly fire, also stops the fleet from treating the aircraft as an enemy. A safeguard becomes the opening for the attack. The title is the premise, written out so a reader can see the trap before the novel springs it. Continue on IFF: Identify Friend or Foe.

Operation Dragon Fire follows an Iraqi terrorist who was admitted to the United States after helping American troops. He recruits a group for coordinated attacks. Targets in New York and Washington are meant to conceal a harder objective: a nuclear strike between the White House and the Capitol. The Dragon Fire troops are called to stop a plan that depends on distraction, timing, and attacks that grow worse while the real target stays hidden. Continue on Operation Dragon Fire.

Beyond Yesterday begins after a nuclear exchange has destroyed most of the world’s population. Survivors learn how to reach into the past. Attempts to replenish humanity with reproductive material fail. They turn to taking people from earlier times, including the pilots of five aircraft that disappeared in the Bermuda Triangle. Each journey leaves consequences in the Atlantic. The mysteries of those disappearances become part of a desperate effort to keep a human future. Continue on Beyond Yesterday.

Sandstorm sends American special operations soldiers into Iran. A rogue Russian admiral has sold Iran nuclear-tipped missiles launched from submarines. The team has to destroy the missiles and the submarines, and it has to bring back evidence of the transfer, evidence that could show how Iran acquired the means to build and launch a nuclear threat against the United States. The published description is a raid plus a proof. Continue on Sandstorm.

Daughter of the Scorpion turns on secret military papers that expose an Iraqi biological weapons program. American intelligence learns the pathogen could mutate and threaten life on a global scale if it is released. Captain Robert Walker leads a rapid response team into the Iraqi desert to find a young woman believed to know where the weapon is hidden. The clock in the published description is that search, not a courtroom drama and not a laboratory procedural. Continue on Daughter of the Scorpion.

Those five are a path, not the whole library. All 21 novels remain listed together, the two trilogies stay in order on Series, and the twelve portfolios stay on Flipbooks.
