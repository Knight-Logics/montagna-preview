IN THE LIBRARY

What the cart remembers, and what it cannot buy

The cart is a list in your browser. It remembers a paperback or a hardback, a quantity, and nothing else until checkout. It does not reserve a copy in a warehouse. It does not tell the author you intend to buy. It does not transmit the list to Knight Logics. If you close the browser’s stored data for this site, the cart is gone, and that is the correct result rather than a failure.

Prices, where they appear, are the IngramSpark paperback and hardback prices used for this design. They can be wrong by the time a seller changes a listing. Terms of use says the seller’s price is the one that counts, and that a missing price is not a free book. Stripe is not connected, so there is no card field and no charge to dispute. If you need a refund, you did not pay it here.

Use the cart as a private shortlist if that helps you compare editions. Use the reading list if you are not ready to choose paper or cloth. The reading list stores titles only. Both lists are explained again on Privacy.

A cart with nothing in it is an invitation back to the shelves, not an error. all 21 novels is the full set. New Release, 2026 collection, and 2025 collection are the author’s groupings. A novel page is where an edition is actually chosen.

The searches this library can honestly meet are historical fantasy and the dual timeline, for Wind Trilogy; and military thriller, spy thriller, espionage, high stakes, action thriller, ensemble cast, and series continuation, for Scorpion Trilogy and the standalone operations. “Mystery thriller books” drew more search interest than sci-fi novels or “fantasy fiction trends” from June 2025 through June 2026, with a peak in October 2025, which is why a thriller reader may arrive here and should then be shown the right shelf. Domestic thriller interest climbed into February 2026, and romantasy remains a separate boom. Neither phrase is a description of these novels, so the pages do not wear those labels. Fantasy was the most written and most requested genre on QueryTracker as of 17 December 2025. Birth of Wind is this library’s fantasy. It is not a invitation to relabel the Iran operations as fantasy because the larger genre is crowded.

Privacy, Terms of use, and Copyright are the three policy pages. They are written for this preview: a local cart that never charges a card, a reading list that stays in the browser, a contact form that does not send, YouTube trailers, and flipbook images hosted here. The author’s official site did not present those policies at its homepage or at /privacy/. These pages do not pretend to govern that site.

The novels most often used as doors are Birth of Wind, Eagle’s Talon, and The Vector. Add one from its own page if you want the cart to mean a specific edition rather than a vague intention.

Five further novels sit one link away from this page. Each note below is the published situation of that book, not a new plot. Follow the one that matches the search you actually typed.

Operation Glass Slipper begins with a scientific gift. A doctor finds a microbe that lives by consuming carbon in crude oil. With a microbiologist, he alters it so the organism can travel through the air and consume carbon pollution. The same discovery is then seen by a small group of terrorists as a path toward a biological weapon. The published description holds both uses in one research project: a repair for the atmosphere, and a weapon made from that repair. Continue on Operation Glass Slipper.

Operation Dragon Claw puts a terrorist group on La Palma. They intend to place explosives along a crack in the Cumbre Vieja volcano. In the novel’s scenario, the collapse would throw an enormous mass of earth into the Atlantic and raise a tsunami. Dragon Fire troops are sent to stop the attack before the consequences leave the island. The published description is careful to call that collapse the novel’s scenario. This preview repeats that caution. It is fiction about a plan, not a forecast. Continue on Operation Dragon Claw.

Trump’s Brass Escalator continues Sandstorm. The president, the vice-president, Special Forces leader Walker, and his female sergeant are in New York to present evidence gathered in Iran to the United Nations. Before they can, robbers attack Trump Tower and take the vice-president hostage. Walker’s team has to break off the larger mission to stop the robbery and save a life at the center of government. Continue on Trump’s Brass Escalator.

The Scorpion Sting begins after Eagle’s Talon. Saddam Hussein sends another group into the United States to kill the Iraqi operative American forces captured, and to kill soldiers involved in that arrest. Walker’s team has to find the new cell. The danger follows the soldiers home. The published description is a retaliation story, which is why the series page treats it as the continuation rather than as a second first chapter. Continue on The Scorpion Sting.

Birth of Wind opens the Wind Trilogy in Japan in 1333. The published description sets a female samurai against a society that will not make a place for her, and it ends with a curse the shogun lays on her sword, binding the warrior and the blade. That is the spine of the novel on this site. The page does not pretend to replace the book, and it does not add battles, names, or a last chapter the published description does not support. Continue on Birth of Wind.

Those five are a path, not the whole library. All 21 novels remain listed together, the two trilogies stay in order on Series, and the twelve portfolios stay on Flipbooks.
