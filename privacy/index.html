Who operates this preview

Knight Logics prepared this website as a design preview for the novels of Stephen Francis Montagna. It is not his official website. His published site remains stephenfrancismontagna.com. That public homepage does not present a privacy policy, and the address /privacy/ on that domain answers that the page is not found. The policies on this preview govern this preview only. They do not speak for the author’s official site, for YouTube, or for any bookseller linked from a novel page.

You can open the preview on the computer where it is built. A review copy is also published at the Knight Logics GitHub Pages address for this project. Every page asks search engines not to index it, and a robots file asks crawlers not to collect the pages. Those are requests, not a lock. Anyone who has the address can open the pages. Do not treat this preview as a private room.

What this preview does not collect

There is no account and no password. The pages as built do not include an advertising pixel or a traffic-analytics tag. The preview does not sell personal information, because it does not receive a copy of your cart, your reading list, or a checkout form. Nothing in the cart is sent to Knight Logics or to the author.

Search on the site loads a catalog file that is already part of the website. That request is for the list of titles. It does not include the books you have saved or the address you might type at checkout.

The reading list

The reading list is stored in local storage in your browser, for this site’s address only. The plus on a book writes that book’s identifier into the list. The same browser can show the list again on a later visit. The preview does not copy the list to a server. You can clear it with the button on this page. You can also remove it by clearing stored data for this site in the browser. Clearing it here does not affect any list you might keep on the author’s official site.

The cart and checkout

The cart is stored in the same kind of local storage. Adding a paperback or a hardback records the book, the edition, and a quantity. The page limits that quantity. Checkout asks for a name, an email address, and a shipping address: street, an optional second line, city, region, postal code, and country. If you submit the form, the preview saves an order record on the device and remembers the newest order in session storage so the same tab can show it. The record holds the items, a subtotal, the address you typed, and the time.

The form does not ask for a card number. Stripe is not connected. The preview does not charge a card, does not calculate shipping, and does not transmit the order to a warehouse, a payment company, or the author. A confirmation that the order was held on the device is the whole of the transaction. It is not a purchase and it is not a reservation with a bookseller. Clearing this site’s stored data removes the cart and any held order.

The contact form

The contact form is shaped for a later connection to Formspree. Until that endpoint is added in the build, submitting the form does not send the name, the email, or the message anywhere. The contact page says so. Do not use the form for a time-sensitive request, a copyright notice, or any message you need a person to receive. This preview does not publish an email address or a telephone number.

Trailers, sellers, and flipbooks

Trailers are films already published on YouTube. On the screening room page, the player is a privacy-enhanced embed from youtube-nocookie.com, so the film can sit in the page. On other pages, a trailer is placed in the player only after you choose to play it. Once that player loads, YouTube receives the request under YouTube’s own policy. This preview does not receive a copy of your YouTube account.

Edition links leave this site for the seller’s pages, including IngramSpark listings used as the source of the prices shown here. A purchase you complete there is a purchase from that seller, under that seller’s privacy policy and terms. This preview does not see the payment.

Where a novel has a flipbook, the pages are images stored with this site. Opening one does not send you to Heyzine and does not open an account on that service. The images were prepared from portfolios the author had already published, so the pages could be turned here. See Copyright for what you may do with those images.

Motion, children, and changes

The pause control in the header can stop the background motion. The preview also follows a device setting that asks for reduced motion. You can return the preview to that device setting from this page. If you set the choice on the page itself, the preference is kept in local storage for this site.

The novels are adult fiction. They include war, terrorism, and, in some books, biological or nuclear danger. This preview is not directed at children, and it does not knowingly collect information from children.

If this preview later gains a working contact form, a payment company, or an account, this policy has to be rewritten first. The sentences above would no longer be true. This text is dated 28 October 2026 and matches the preview as built on that date. The related pages are Terms of use and Copyright. Questions about a novel itself belong with the author, through the channel he chooses on his official site. This page cannot forward them.

IN THE LIBRARY

From this policy back into the library

The policy above is the rule. This note only connects it to the shelves, so a person who arrived from a novel can get back. all 21 novels holds every title. Wind Trilogy and Scorpion Trilogy are the two orders. flipbooks is where the twelve portfolios are listed. The cart and the reading list mentioned in the policy are the cart and the reading list. The form that does not send is contact.

The searches this library can honestly meet are historical fantasy and the dual timeline, for Wind Trilogy; and military thriller, spy thriller, espionage, high stakes, action thriller, ensemble cast, and series continuation, for Scorpion Trilogy and the standalone operations. “Mystery thriller books” drew more search interest than sci-fi novels or “fantasy fiction trends” from June 2025 through June 2026, with a peak in October 2025, which is why a thriller reader may arrive here and should then be shown the right shelf. Domestic thriller interest climbed into February 2026, and romantasy remains a separate boom. Neither phrase is a description of these novels, so the pages do not wear those labels. Fantasy was the most written and most requested genre on QueryTracker as of 17 December 2025. Birth of Wind is this library’s fantasy. It is not a invitation to relabel the Iran operations as fantasy because the larger genre is crowded.

Privacy, Terms of use, and Copyright are the three policy pages. They are written for this preview: a local cart that never charges a card, a reading list that stays in the browser, a contact form that does not send, YouTube trailers, and flipbook images hosted here. The author’s official site did not present those policies at its homepage or at /privacy/. These pages do not pretend to govern that site.

Start a book from here if you came for the policy and stayed for the library: Birth of Wind, Eagle’s Talon, or The Vector.

Five further novels sit one link away from this page. Each note below is the published situation of that book, not a new plot. Follow the one that matches the search you actually typed.

Beyond Yesterday begins after a nuclear exchange has destroyed most of the world’s population. Survivors learn how to reach into the past. Attempts to replenish humanity with reproductive material fail. They turn to taking people from earlier times, including the pilots of five aircraft that disappeared in the Bermuda Triangle. Each journey leaves consequences in the Atlantic. The mysteries of those disappearances become part of a desperate effort to keep a human future. Continue on Beyond Yesterday.

Sandstorm sends American special operations soldiers into Iran. A rogue Russian admiral has sold Iran nuclear-tipped missiles launched from submarines. The team has to destroy the missiles and the submarines, and it has to bring back evidence of the transfer, evidence that could show how Iran acquired the means to build and launch a nuclear threat against the United States. The published description is a raid plus a proof. Continue on Sandstorm.

Daughter of the Scorpion turns on secret military papers that expose an Iraqi biological weapons program. American intelligence learns the pathogen could mutate and threaten life on a global scale if it is released. Captain Robert Walker leads a rapid response team into the Iraqi desert to find a young woman believed to know where the weapon is hidden. The clock in the published description is that search, not a courtroom drama and not a laboratory procedural. Continue on Daughter of the Scorpion.

The Wind’s Breathe is the second novel. Centuries after Birth of Wind, the cursed sword is found in Japan in 1996. Its new owner turns Wind’s samurai abilities against rivals in the construction industry. The published description is that specific: not a return to the fourteenth-century war, but the same binding put to work in a modern commercial fight. The title on the author’s materials uses this spelling, and so does this preview. Continue on The Wind’s Breathe.

The Vulture Will Lead You to Death follows terrorists who enter the United States from Cuba with a plan against the Port of Miami. They know a supertanker is docked there. They do not know that a ship carrying military supplies is stationed nearby, and that its presence could magnify the blast. The published description stops on that unawareness. The novel is the working out of an attack whose second weapon is the port itself. Continue on The Vulture Will Lead You to Death.

Those five are a path, not the whole library. All 21 novels remain listed together, the two trilogies stay in order on Series, and the twelve portfolios stay on Flipbooks.
