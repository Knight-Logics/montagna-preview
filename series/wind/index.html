Begin with Birth of Wind ↗

The Wind Trilogy moves through three settings, linked by a warrior’s life and the curse that binds her to a sword. Reading in sequence lets the meaning of that bond unfold before the blade reaches each new owner.

Begin with the female samurai in Japan in 1333. The second novel moves to Japan in 1996, where the rediscovered sword carries Wind’s abilities into a new kind of conflict. Night Wind follows the blade to the United States with an FBI agent, extending the warrior’s obligation across the Pacific.

READER’S GUIDE

The Wind Trilogy: Japanese mythology, a female samurai and seven centuries

The Wind Trilogy by Stephen Francis Montagna follows Wind, a female samurai of 1333 Japan, and the cursed sword that carries her duty to 1996 Japan and the United States.

The Wind Trilogy is historical fantasy in three novels. Birth of Wind follows a female samurai through the male-dominated Japan of 1333 to the shogun’s curse that binds her to her sword. The Wind’s Breathe finds the sword in Japan in 1996, where its new owner uses her skills against his business rivals. Night Wind puts it in the hands of an FBI agent and carries it to the United States, where Wind must protect whoever holds the blade.

The compilation review published for the series by Pacific Book Review calls it an ambitious blend of Japanese mythology, feudal history and modern-day thriller, in which Shinto deities, samurai codes and institutions such as the FBI meet. Its full text is on this site, along with a review of each novel.

Questions about the Wind Trilogy

How many books are in the Wind Trilogy?

Three: Birth of Wind, The Wind’s Breathe and Night Wind, read in that order.

Is the Wind Trilogy based on Japanese mythology?

It draws on it. The first novel opens with a prologue in the creation myth of Izanagi and Izanami before moving into the history of 1333, and the reviews describe Shinto deities at work across the series. Wind herself is fictional.

Is there a Wind Trilogy trailer?

Yes: a 71-second film for the whole trilogy and the official Birth of Wind trailer, both in the screening room.
