The review documents for Eagle’s Talon

The images are the review material already published on the author’s website for Eagle’s Talon. Knight Logics did not write a new notice and did not invent a rating. The guide under the scans returns you to the novel and to the shelf it belongs on.

Eagle’s Talon is the opening novel of the Scorpion Trilogy. In the published story, Saddam Hussein sends a terrorist group to assassinate the American president in an effort to end sanctions. Robert Walker and his Special Forces troops are sent to stop the attack, and the mission then takes them into Iraq to capture the terrorist leader and bring him to the United States for trial.

Military-thriller readers, in a count of 1,737 trope mentions over the 28 weeks to 7 September 2026, keep naming action thriller, page-turner, spy thriller, series continuation, ensemble cast, high stakes, and espionage. Eagle’s Talon sits in that language: a team, a pursuit, and a political purpose under the operation. A separate report of Google search data from June 2025 through June 2026 found “mystery thriller books” drawing more interest than “sci-fi novels” or the phrase “fantasy fiction trends,” with a peak in October 2025. This novel is the military kind of that search, not a domestic thriller and not a mystery set in a house.

Start the trilogy here. The Scorpion Sting is the retaliation that follows the capture. Daughter of the Scorpion is the later biological threat. Reading them out of order turns a series continuation into three disconnected raids.

Five further novels sit one link away from this page. Each note below is the published situation of that book, not a new plot. Follow the one that matches the search you actually typed.

Operation Dragon Claw puts a terrorist group on La Palma. They intend to place explosives along a crack in the Cumbre Vieja volcano. In the novel’s scenario, the collapse would throw an enormous mass of earth into the Atlantic and raise a tsunami. Dragon Fire troops are sent to stop the attack before the consequences leave the island. The published description is careful to call that collapse the novel’s scenario. This preview repeats that caution. It is fiction about a plan, not a forecast. Continue on Operation Dragon Claw.

Trump’s Brass Escalator continues Sandstorm. The president, the vice-president, Special Forces leader Walker, and his female sergeant are in New York to present evidence gathered in Iran to the United Nations. Before they can, robbers attack Trump Tower and take the vice-president hostage. Walker’s team has to break off the larger mission to stop the robbery and save a life at the center of government. Continue on Trump’s Brass Escalator.

The Scorpion Sting begins after Eagle’s Talon. Saddam Hussein sends another group into the United States to kill the Iraqi operative American forces captured, and to kill soldiers involved in that arrest. Walker’s team has to find the new cell. The danger follows the soldiers home. The published description is a retaliation story, which is why the series page treats it as the continuation rather than as a second first chapter. Continue on The Scorpion Sting.

Birth of Wind opens the Wind Trilogy in Japan in 1333. The published description sets a female samurai against a society that will not make a place for her, and it ends with a curse the shogun lays on her sword, binding the warrior and the blade. That is the spine of the novel on this site. The page does not pretend to replace the book, and it does not add battles, names, or a last chapter the published description does not support. Continue on Birth of Wind.

The Paradise of the Devil begins with a Cuban colonel who has lost faith in the condition of the military. He confronts Castro, kills him, and takes command of the island, expecting the people to support the takeover. A civilian army rises against his troops instead. Under that civil war sits a secret left from the Cuban Missile Crisis, and the secret pulls the United States into a confrontation that does not stay in Cuba. Continue on The Paradise of the Devil.

Those five are a path, not the whole library. All 21 novels remain listed together, the two trilogies stay in order on Series, and the twelve portfolios stay on Flipbooks.
