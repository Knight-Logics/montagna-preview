IN THE LIBRARY

The review documents for Operation Dragon Fire

The images are the review material already published on the author’s website for Operation Dragon Fire. Knight Logics did not write a new notice and did not invent a rating. The guide under the scans returns you to the novel and to the shelf it belongs on.

Operation Dragon Fire follows an Iraqi terrorist who was admitted to the United States after helping American troops. He recruits a group for coordinated attacks. Targets in New York and Washington are meant to conceal a harder objective: a nuclear strike between the White House and the Capitol. The Dragon Fire troops are called to stop a plan that depends on distraction, timing, and attacks that grow worse while the real target stays hidden.

Action thriller, high stakes, and ensemble cast are the military-thriller phrases that fit a named special unit and a concealed nuclear aim. Series continuation is a weaker fit unless you are already reading the Dragon Fire operations beside Bio-Level Three and Operation Dragon Claw. Those three share a unit more than they share one plot. Spy thriller readers will recognize the misdirection. Domestic-thriller readers, following a search that climbed sharply into early 2026, will not find a household mystery here.

God’s Sword of Justice is the other nuclear countdown, at Indian Point rather than on the National Mall. Sandstorm is the mission into Iran for submarine-launched missiles, and Trump’s Brass Escalator continues that evidence into New York.

Operation Dragon Fire sits on the military and geopolitical shelf. Five further novels sit one link away from this page. Each note below is the published situation of that book, not a new plot. Follow the one that matches the search you actually typed.

The Scorpion Sting begins after Eagle’s Talon. Saddam Hussein sends another group into the United States to kill the Iraqi operative American forces captured, and to kill soldiers involved in that arrest. Walker’s team has to find the new cell. The danger follows the soldiers home. The published description is a retaliation story, which is why the series page treats it as the continuation rather than as a second first chapter. Continue on The Scorpion Sting.

Birth of Wind opens the Wind Trilogy in Japan in 1333. The published description sets a female samurai against a society that will not make a place for her, and it ends with a curse the shogun lays on her sword, binding the warrior and the blade. That is the spine of the novel on this site. The page does not pretend to replace the book, and it does not add battles, names, or a last chapter the published description does not support. Continue on Birth of Wind.

The Paradise of the Devil begins with a Cuban colonel who has lost faith in the condition of the military. He confronts Castro, kills him, and takes command of the island, expecting the people to support the takeover. A civilian army rises against his troops instead. Under that civil war sits a secret left from the Cuban Missile Crisis, and the secret pulls the United States into a confrontation that does not stay in Cuba. Continue on The Paradise of the Devil.

The Warning of Ramses starts from Egypt’s dependence on the Nile. Ethiopia builds a dam on the Blue Nile and sharply reduces the water reaching Egypt. The dispute moves toward war. Libya joins Egypt. The confrontation also involves the United States and the dam project. A river becomes a regional crisis. That is the published spine, and it is enough to tell you the novel is about water, alliances, and the point where a resource war stops being a negotiation. Continue on The Warning of Ramses.

Operation Glass Slipper begins with a scientific gift. A doctor finds a microbe that lives by consuming carbon in crude oil. With a microbiologist, he alters it so the organism can travel through the air and consume carbon pollution. The same discovery is then seen by a small group of terrorists as a path toward a biological weapon. The published description holds both uses in one research project: a repair for the atmosphere, and a weapon made from that repair. Continue on Operation Glass Slipper.
