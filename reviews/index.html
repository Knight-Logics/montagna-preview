IN THE LIBRARY

How to read the review archive

The review archive is a set of scans from the author’s existing website. The credits are the ones printed on the artwork. Knight Logics did not write replacement reviews, did not add star ratings, and did not verify each reviewer’s identity beyond what the artwork shows. The note on the archive page says that plainly. This guide exists so the archive is still a readable page, with a way back to each novel, rather than a folder of pictures with no shelf.

Open a scan, read the credit on it, then use the novel link. The novels with documents include both trilogies and many of the operations: Wind, Birth of Wind, The Wind’s Breathe, Night Wind, the Scorpion set, and a run of thrillers through The Vector, Sandstorm, Cold Fire, and Beyond Yesterday. If a novel is missing from the archive, it is missing because that document was not on the source site, not because a review was deleted here.

These scans are not the flipbooks. A flipbook is the portfolio of a novel, turned as pages, and it lives on the novel page and in flipbooks. A review scan is someone else’s notice, kept as artwork. Copyright says you may look and may not strip the author’s name off the art and republish it as an endorsement this preview invented.

The searches this library can honestly meet are historical fantasy and the dual timeline, for Wind Trilogy; and military thriller, spy thriller, espionage, high stakes, action thriller, ensemble cast, and series continuation, for Scorpion Trilogy and the standalone operations. “Mystery thriller books” drew more search interest than sci-fi novels or “fantasy fiction trends” from June 2025 through June 2026, with a peak in October 2025, which is why a thriller reader may arrive here and should then be shown the right shelf. Domestic thriller interest climbed into February 2026, and romantasy remains a separate boom. Neither phrase is a description of these novels, so the pages do not wear those labels. Fantasy was the most written and most requested genre on QueryTracker as of 17 December 2025. Birth of Wind is this library’s fantasy. It is not a invitation to relabel the Iran operations as fantasy because the larger genre is crowded.

Privacy, Terms of use, and Copyright are the three policy pages. They are written for this preview: a local cart that never charges a card, a reading list that stays in the browser, a contact form that does not send, YouTube trailers, and flipbook images hosted here. The author’s official site did not present those policies at its homepage or at /privacy/. These pages do not pretend to govern that site.

Five further novels sit one link away from this page. Each note below is the published situation of that book, not a new plot. Follow the one that matches the search you actually typed.

Eagle’s Talon is the opening novel of the Scorpion Trilogy. In the published story, Saddam Hussein sends a terrorist group to assassinate the American president in an effort to end sanctions. Robert Walker and his Special Forces troops are sent to stop the attack, and the mission then takes them into Iraq to capture the terrorist leader and bring him to the United States for trial. Continue on Eagle’s Talon.

Night Wind closes the trilogy’s published arc on this site. The sword is discovered by the FBI. The agent who takes responsibility brings it to the United States and learns it is not an ordinary weapon. Wind remains bound to protect the person who possesses it. That duty, not a new origin story, is what the third novel puts in a new country. Continue on Night Wind.

The Vector is the preview’s plain example of a geopolitical thriller. Iran answers a war with a covert biological attack on the United States. Eight volunteers are fitted with false teeth that hold a deadly agent and are sent through Mexico toward eight American cities. Their orders are to release the agent and spread the infection before it kills them. The weapon in the published description is a person, not a missile. Continue on The Vector.

God’s Sword of Justice places terrorists in the control room of the Indian Point nuclear power plant in New York. They shut off cooling water to the reactor. The published description gives the region a nine-hour window before a catastrophic meltdown and the contamination that would follow. Walker’s elite soldiers have to stop the attackers before the plant itself becomes the weapon. Continue on God’s Sword of Justice.

Bio-Level Three sends specialized Dragon Fire troops into the Libyan desert. A rebel-led military command is secretly building twin chemical and biological factories. American intelligence finds the project. The president orders the factories destroyed before they can operate. The published description is a preemption story: stop the buildings before the weapons exist, not investigate a crime after a city is already sick. Continue on Bio-Level Three.

Those five are a path, not the whole library. All 21 novels remain listed together, the two trilogies stay in order on Series, and the twelve portfolios stay on Flipbooks.
