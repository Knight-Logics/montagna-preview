## The review

Reviewer: Jon Carlisle  
Outlet: Pacific Book Review

Stephen Francis Montagna’s Night Wind, the culminating volume in the Wind Trilogy, delivers a bold and imaginative fusion of mythology, supernatural intrigue, and modern-day suspense. Building upon the expansive historical and mythological groundwork of the earlier books, this final installment shifts into a more dynamic and genre-blending narrative—one that intertwines ancient samurai legend with contemporary crime fiction.

From its opening pages, Night Wind establishes a striking premise. The spirit of Wind—a fierce and tragic female samurai bound by a cursed katana—is brought before a divine council of Japanese deities to answer for her defiance. This celestial tribunal, featuring figures such as Izanagi, Izanami, and Fujin, reinforces Montagna’s continued commitment to mythological authenticity while elevating the stakes of the story to a cosmic level. The author’s vivid depiction of this supernatural courtroom creates an atmosphere rich with tension, honor, and philosophical inquiry.

As the narrative unfolds, Montagna introduces a compelling contrast by shifting into a modern setting, where an FBI investigation into a brutal (murder) becomes entangled with the reawakening of ancient forces. The discovery of a seemingly worthless samurai sword— later revealed to possess extraordinary power—acts as a bridge between past and present. This transition is handled with creativity, allowing the story to evolve into a hybrid of historical fantasy and investigative thriller.

One of the novel’s most distinctive strengths is its central character, Wind. As a female samurai bound by duty, curse, and honor, she embodies both strength and tragedy. Her existence—controlled by whoever wields her sword—raises compelling questions about free will, loyalty, and identity. Montagna effectively uses her character to explore deeper moral conflicts, particularly as she navigates both divine judgment and human manipulation.

Stylistically, Montagna maintains his signature epic tone while incorporating faster-paced, dialogue-driven sequences in the modern storyline. This dual narrative approach adds variety and energy, keeping readers engaged across different timelines and perspectives. The author’s descriptive passages remain vivid, particularly in scenes involving supernatural manifestations and violent confrontations.

However, the novel’s ambitious scope may present challenges for some readers. The blending of mythological, historical, and contemporary elements—while innovative—can occasionally feel dense or overwhelming. Additionally, the dialogue in certain sections leans heavily on exposition, which may slow the pacing for those seeking a more streamlined narrative.

Despite these minor drawbacks, Night Wind succeeds as a powerful and imaginative conclusion to the trilogy. Montagna’s willingness to merge genres and explore complex themes sets the work apart, offering readers a story that is both intellectually engaging and narratively bold.

### Pacific Book Review Verdict:

Night Wind is a compelling and inventive finale that successfully bridges ancient mythology with modern suspense. With its unique premise, strong central character, and expansive vision, it delivers a satisfying conclusion for readers invested in the Wind Trilogy.
