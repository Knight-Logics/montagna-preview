THE BOOK AT A GLANCE

SOURCE PAGE 05

The Warning of Ramses begins with a warning delivered more than three thousand years ago.

Pharaoh Ramses II stands beside the Nile and declares that Egypt's survival depends upon the uninterrupted flow of its sacred river. His warning is not merely religious—it becomes a prophecy of political survival.

Centuries later, the nightmare imagined by Ramses begins to take shape.

Ethiopia proposes diverting a major portion of the waters flowing from Lake Tana toward the Blue Nile in order to irrigate its drought-stricken lowlands and feed its growing population. For Ethiopia, the water is essential to survival and development. For Egypt, any substantial reduction in the Nile's flow represents an existential threat.

What begins as a diplomatic dispute rapidly becomes an international crisis.

Egypt warns that it will go to war rather than allow its Nile water to be threatened. The United Nations attempts to mediate. The United States offers engineers, money and technical assistance to Ethiopia. For a moment, diplomacy appears capable of preventing disaster.

But beneath the public negotiations, another game is being played.

SOURCE PAGE 06

Libya and other regional forces begin manipulating events, staging attacks and creating political crises designed to push nations toward confrontation. Egypt undergoes a political transformation, secret military preparations accelerate, and the Ethiopian dam becomes the center of a widening geopolitical struggle.

American engineers and military personnel are dispatched to Ethiopia to help construct and protect the project. Colonel Edward "Popeye" Campanelli becomes one of the novel's principal military figures, overseeing American personnel while increasingly finding himself caught between engineering, diplomacy and war.

As provocations multiply, the conflict spreads beyond the dam.

Chad becomes a pawn. Sudan becomes threatened. Israel becomes drawn into the widening crisis. Iraq and Libya pursue their own strategic ambitions. Secret alliances and covert operations transform what was supposed to be a water project into the fuse of a much larger war.

Eventually, diplomacy collapses.

The United States finds itself fighting across multiple fronts. Advanced aircraft, naval forces, intelligence operations, ground troops and covert technology become central to the struggle. The conflict escalates beyond conventional warfare, culminating in devastating biological and nuclear attacks.

The human cost is catastrophic.

SOURCE PAGE 07

More than 70,000 American soldiers are estimated killed, with thousands more expected to die from radiation exposure and biological attacks. Enormous portions of the affected region become uninhabitable.

For Colonel Campanelli, the war becomes intensely personal when Captain Renee Mendoza—his military colleague and lover—is killed. He returns home physically and emotionally devastated, ultimately relieved of active command and promoted to general. Yet the memory of Mendoza remains with him, suggesting that surviving a war can be almost as difficult as fighting one.

The ancient warning of Ramses has therefore become horrifyingly relevant.

The Nile was never simply a river.

It was the line between survival and extinction.

SOURCE PAGE 12

The novel's modern crisis begins in the mid-1990s.

Ethiopia, suffering from drought and struggling to feed its population, proposes diverting a large percentage of the water flowing from Lake Tana into the Blue Nile. Ethiopia argues that it needs the water for irrigation and food production.

Egypt sees the proposal very differently.

To Egyptian leaders, losing a substantial amount of Blue Nile water threatens the survival of a nation whose population is concentrated along the river.

The dispute reaches the United Nations.

Egyptian Ambassador Mohammed Kheir delivers an increasingly forceful warning that Egypt will fight if Ethiopia interferes with the Nile's flow. Other countries begin taking sides. Sudan supports Ethiopia, while several nations express sympathy for Egypt. The United States, Britain and France attempt to redirect the confrontation toward a peaceful solution by offering technical assistance to Ethiopia.

For a brief moment, war appears to have been avoided.

But Kheir does not believe the danger has disappeared.

He is correct.

SOURCE PAGE 13

Behind the diplomatic façade, Libyan Ambassador Kamal begins working with Egyptian political forces. The two men become increasingly involved in secret plans designed to manipulate regional conflicts and manufacture circumstances that will shift international opinion.

The political situation in Egypt itself changes dramatically.

President Hosni Mubarak is removed, and Faisal Sadat, presented as the son of former Egyptian President Anwar Sadat, assumes the presidency. Kheir discovers that the new regime intends to allow Ethiopia to proceed with the dam while Egypt quietly prepares for a future confrontation.

The strategy is deliberately long-term.

Egypt intends to use the time gained by allowing construction to proceed to prepare its military forces.

The United States meanwhile commits substantial resources to the Ethiopian project.

American Army engineers are sent to Ethiopia. Captain Edward "Popeye" Campanelli assumes responsibility for the American engineering and security operation. Campanelli is a capable but unconventional officer whose personality frequently puts him at odds with military bureaucracy.

SOURCE PAGE 14

The project becomes increasingly militarized.

American engineers construct the dam while military personnel provide security. Ethiopian officials warn Campanelli about instability in neighboring Chad and the possibility of Libyan intervention. The American command responds by sending additional personnel and highly trained forces under the nominal cover of civilian protection.

At the center of the developing military storyline is Campanelli's relationship with Captain Renee Mendoza, alongside other American officers including John White.

Meanwhile, Mohammed Kheir becomes increasingly trapped between competing loyalties.

He believes Egypt must protect the Nile, but he also recognizes that the political forces surrounding him are becoming increasingly dangerous.

Libya begins conducting covert attacks designed to make other countries appear responsible. One operation targets the American/Ethiopian dam project while the perpetrators attempt to make it appear that forces connected to Chad are responsible. The strategy works initially, producing international condemnation of Chad.

SOURCE PAGE 15

The conspirators then plan additional attacks.

The objective is not simply military victory.

It is to manipulate international perception.

The novel's political thriller dimension intensifies as Egypt, Libya and Iraq develop overlapping plans involving Chad, Sudan, Israel and the United States. Iraqi Ambassador Mohammed Nimiri reveals that Russian defectors have supplied Libya with advanced weapons and that Libya and Iraq have broader plans for regional warfare.

The dam remains the catalyst.

As construction progresses, Mohammed Kheir watches the American engineering operation from Egypt. The sight of American engineers rerouting the waters of Lake Tana reinforces his fear that Egypt is approaching the moment from which there will be no return.

The military situation deteriorates.

Attacks occur against the dam and surrounding installations. Chad becomes increasingly destabilized. Sudan prepares for possible invasion. American forces build a major operational base in the region.

Eventually the conflict expands into direct warfare.

SOURCE PAGE 16

Egypt begins preparing its forces in the Sinai. Israel responds when Egyptian military forces move into the desert. American forces become involved as the wider regional conflict threatens American interests and allies.

The fighting escalates rapidly.

Air battles, naval engagements, armored warfare and ground operations spread across the region. American carrier groups become major components of the war effort. Advanced aircraft and intelligence systems are used to locate and destroy enemy forces.

The conflict eventually reaches Libya.

American forces attack Libyan positions from multiple directions while Libyan forces strike American naval vessels. Several American warships are badly damaged or sunk, causing the United States to escalate its response.

American intelligence eventually discovers that Libyan forces have been exploiting compromised satellite information to anticipate American operations. Once that intelligence channel is shut down, the balance of the air war changes dramatically.

The American military begins mounting increasingly devastating attacks.

But victory comes at an extraordinary price.

SOURCE PAGE 17

The war ultimately crosses the threshold into biological and nuclear warfare.

The destruction becomes almost unimaginable.

American forces suffer enormous casualties, while large portions of North Africa and the Middle East are devastated. The manuscript states that more than 70,000 American soldiers are killed by biological or nuclear attacks, with additional deaths expected from radiation exposure. A fifth of the world's surface in the affected region is described as becoming devoid of human and animal life for a century.

For Campanelli, the catastrophe becomes personal.

Captain Renee Mendoza is killed.

The Colonel's grief is overwhelming. He remains beside her remains before the survivors are evacuated.

The war has transformed him.

When Campanelli eventually returns home, he is promoted to One-Star General but is removed from active command because of the devastating psychological impact of Mendoza's death.

He returns to his wife, but the manuscript makes clear that he has not truly returned emotionally.

SOURCE PAGE 18

Mendoza's memory has become part of him.

The novel closes on a deeply human note after the enormous geopolitical catastrophe: a soldier survives the war, but survival does not mean escape from its consequences.

The warning Ramses issued thousands of years earlier has been fulfilled in the most catastrophic way imaginable.

COLONEL EDWARD "POPEYE" CAMPANELLI

Campanelli is an experienced officer whose unconventional personality and refusal to blindly conform to military bureaucracy have repeatedly stalled his career. He is placed in charge of the American engineering/security operation in Ethiopia and eventually becomes deeply involved in the regional war.

His storyline gives the novel a strong ground-level military perspective.

He is not simply commanding troops; he is watching an international crisis grow around him.

His relationship with Renee Mendoza also gives the increasingly massive war a personal emotional center.

BOOK REVIEW

SOURCE PAGE 46

An ancient pharaoh’s warning ripples across three millennia to ignite a modern geopolitical firestorm in Stephen Francis Montagna’s gripping technothriller, The Warning of Ramses. Blending meticulous historical and geographical detail with high-stakes international espionage, Montagna weaves a cautionary tale that explores a terrifyingly plausible scenario: the weaponization of the world’s most vital resource—water.

The story hinges on the haunting words of Ramses II, who decreed that if the life-giving waters of the Nile should ever stop flowing, Egypt would wither and die. This ancient prophecy transforms into a modern nightmare when an unrelenting fourteen-year drought pushes Ethiopia to construct a massive dam on the Blue Nile feeder river. Intending to divert 80% of the water to save its own starving population, Ethiopia inadvertently suffocates its northern neighbor, cutting off the lifeline that sustains 95% of Egypt’s people.

Montagna excels at setting a tense, authentic stage. The opening chapters read like a masterclass in hydropolitics, establishing the dense web of African boundaries and the fragile ecosystems dependent on the Nile’s volcanic silt and heavy rains. This deep dive into geography pays off brilliantly when the narrative shifts to the pressure-cooker environment of the United Nations Security Council. Here, the author captures the volatile friction of international diplomacy. Readers are thrust into a world of fiery debates, fractured alliances, and backroom deals as Egyptian Ambassador Mohammed Kheir desperately tries to avert a catastrophic war while his own country undergoes a radical political coup behind his back.

What sets The Warning of Ramses apart is its complex character web and the terrifying scope of its antagonists. As Egypt aligns with a volatile Libyan regime, the plot spirals into a global chess match involving rogue Russian scientists, advanced anti-ship weaponry, and a terrifying arsenal of nuclear warheads. The tension is palpable as Ambassador Kheir finds himself trapped between his love for his country and the mad ambitions of dictators plotting an apocalyptic restructuring of global power.

With a sharp eye for military logistics, political maneuvering, and the looming threat of resource scarcity, Montagna delivers a fast-paced thriller that is as intellectually stimulating as it is suspenseful. The Warning of Ramses is a stark, thrilling reminder that the wars of the future will not be fought over lines on a map, but for the very fluid of life itself. It is a must-read for fans of Tom Clancy, political intrigue, and military realism.
