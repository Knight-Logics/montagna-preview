PROFESSIONAL STORY SYNOPSIS

SOURCE PAGE 10

IFF: Identify Friend or Foe

SOURCE PAGE 11

At the center of the American storyline is Captain Lawrence "Larry" Benvenuti, commander of the Truman, together with Commander Armando Sanchez, Major Christian Ferratti, and Major Mary Armstrong.

Ferratti and Armstrong become particularly important to the story. They are skilled fighter pilots responsible for protecting the carrier, while another pilot, Lieutenant Stephen Steinbach, is viewed by Benvenuti as an unpredictable liability.

The novel then shifts toward Saudi Arabia, where American and Saudi forces conduct increasingly sophisticated flight exercises. The Americans begin developing relationships with Saudi pilots, including Major Heshmollah Nohghassr.

But Nohghassr is not what he appears to be.

He has secretly been planted within the Saudi military structure as part of an Iranian operation. Iranian leadership has invested years in creating his identity and position, allowing him to operate as a supposedly trusted Saudi officer.

The deception becomes even more dangerous because Nohghassr understands the very American technology that is supposed to protect the carrier.

SOURCE PAGE 12

THE IFF PROBLEM

SOURCE PAGE 13

Nohghassr exploits the confusion.

He deliberately behaves like a pilot experiencing an emergency. He approaches the carrier while maintaining the appearance of a damaged friendly aircraft that needs assistance. Major Ferratti is eventually placed in the position of escorting the apparently crippled Saudi aircraft toward the Truman.

The deception works.

As the American pilots concentrate on helping the "friendly" aircraft, Nohghassr moves closer to the carrier.

The final confrontation becomes a tragic demonstration of the novel's central premise: the system designed to prevent Americans from killing their own people has been manipulated into preventing Americans from defending themselves.

Nohghassr's Hornet ultimately crashes into the Truman, striking the carrier's bomb-storage area and triggering catastrophic explosions. The carrier is torn apart and engulfed in fire. Nohghassr is killed in the attack, but his mission succeeds.

The surviving American pilots watch helplessly as the carrier burns.

SOURCE PAGE 14

Major Ferratti and Major Armstrong survive the immediate attack, while American ships move into the waters around the destroyed carrier to rescue survivors. The tragedy leaves Ferratti devastated and determined to see those responsible held accountable.

The surviving forces are ordered to regroup rather than immediately launch a retaliatory strike. Major Ferratti reluctantly follows orders and departs toward Bahrain, leaving the burning Truman behind. His final salute to the dying carrier becomes one of the novel's strongest emotional images.

IN THE LIBRARY

Turning the pages of IFF: Identify Friend or Foe

The portfolio of IFF: Identify Friend or Foe is the set of pages in the reader above. This guide stays under the reader so the book can still be searched, shelved, and connected to the rest of the library.

IFF: Identify Friend or Foe turns on a stolen aircraft. An Iranian pilot takes an F-18 and turns it against an American carrier in the Persian Gulf. The friendly identification signal, the system built to stop friendly fire, also stops the fleet from treating the aircraft as an enemy. A safeguard becomes the opening for the attack. The title is the premise, written out so a reader can see the trap before the novel springs it.

Spy thriller and espionage are the phrases military-thriller readers already use, and they fit this book more tightly than they fit a straight infantry raid. Action thriller still applies once the aircraft is in the air. The wider mystery-thriller search interest, higher than sci-fi or fantasy-trend queries through mid-2026, is the door many readers walk through. The room they enter here is a carrier and a misidentified plane, not a clue puzzle.

Pair it with The Warning of Ramses if you want two different ways a system can fail a country, and with The Vector if you want the covert biological answer to a war with Iran. They are three novels, not one serial.

Bio-Level Three sends specialized Dragon Fire troops into the Libyan desert. A rebel-led military command is secretly building twin chemical and biological factories. The published description is a preemption story: stop the buildings before the weapons exist, not investigate a crime after a city is already sick. Continue on Bio-Level Three.
