MARKET APPEAL: BIRTH OF WIND BY STEPHEN FRANCIS MONTAGNA

Birth of Wind stands out in the historical fiction genre through its compelling and empowering central character—Wind, a female samurai who defies societal expectations and rises to greatness in a world dominated by men.

Target Audience

Unique Selling Points

• A Powerful Female-Led Samurai Story Unlike traditional samurai narratives, this novel centers on a woman who not only enters this world—but excels in it.

• Empowerment and Representation Wind’s journey resonates with modern audiences, offering a story of resilience, equality, and breaking barriers.

• Character First, History Second While grounded in historical events, the story is driven by Wind’s personal journey—making it more relatable and emotionally engaging.

• Cinematic and Inspirational With intense battles, emotional stakes, and a heroic arc, the story has strong potential for adaptation and broad audience appeal.

EXCERPT FROM BIRTH OF WIND BY STEPHEN FRANCIS MONTAGNA

SOURCE PAGE 11

Steel met steel with a sharp crack that echoed across the yard. The force traveled through her arms, but she held firm, turning the strike aside with precision born not of strength—but of discipline.

The laughter stopped.

Another strike came—faster this time.

She answered it.

Then another.

And another.

Each movement was controlled, deliberate, undeniable.

The men who had mocked her now watched in silence.

Wind did not smile.

She did not speak.

She simply fought.

Because this was never about proving them wrong.

It was about proving herself right.

And as the final blow rang out and her opponent staggered back, something shifted in the air.

Not acceptance.

Not yet.

But something close.

Wind lowered her blade, her breath steady, her gaze unbroken.

They had told her she did not belong.

But the sword in her hand—and the silence that followed—told a different story. It simply bore witness.

IN THE LIBRARY

Turning the pages of Birth of Wind

The portfolio of Birth of Wind is the set of pages in the reader above. This guide stays under the reader so the book can still be searched, shelved, and connected to the rest of the library.

Birth of Wind opens the Wind Trilogy in Japan in 1333. The published description sets a female samurai against a society that will not make a place for her, and it ends with a curse the shogun lays on her sword, binding the warrior and the blade. That is the spine of the novel on this site. The page does not pretend to replace the book, and it does not add battles, names, or a last chapter the published description does not support.

Readers looking for historical fantasy keep meeting the phrase dual timeline. A market snapshot of that lane dated 26 October 2026 named the dual timeline as the signal then rising. This trilogy is built that way on purpose: the first novel is the fourteenth century, and the later novels carry the same sword into 1996 and then across the Pacific. Calling the book a European medieval romance would be the wrong search. The hero’s journey is a fair description of the shape, which is the phrase rising in the broader medieval lane of that same snapshot, but the world on the page is Japan.

Fantasy was also the genre both most written and most requested on QueryTracker as of 17 December 2025. That demand is crowded. Birth of Wind is not part of the romantasy boom, and this page will not borrow that label. It is historical fantasy with a military life at the center. If that is the shelf you came for, start here, then go forward. Do not start with the modern novels and hope the curse will explain itself.

Five further novels sit one link away from this page. Each note below is the published situation of that book, not a new plot. Follow the one that matches the search you actually typed.

Beyond Yesterday begins after a nuclear exchange has destroyed most of the world’s population. Survivors learn how to reach into the past. Attempts to replenish humanity with reproductive material fail. They turn to taking people from earlier times, including the pilots of five aircraft that disappeared in the Bermuda Triangle. Each journey leaves consequences in the Atlantic. The mysteries of those disappearances become part of a desperate effort to keep a human future. Continue on Beyond Yesterday.

Sandstorm sends American special operations soldiers into Iran. A rogue Russian admiral has sold Iran nuclear-tipped missiles launched from submarines. The team has to destroy the missiles and the submarines, and it has to bring back evidence of the transfer, evidence that could show how Iran acquired the means to build and launch a nuclear threat against the United States. The published description is a raid plus a proof. Continue on Sandstorm.

Daughter of the Scorpion turns on secret military papers that expose an Iraqi biological weapons program. American intelligence learns the pathogen could mutate and threaten life on a global scale if it is released. Captain Robert Walker leads a rapid response team into the Iraqi desert to find a young woman believed to know where the weapon is hidden. The clock in the published description is that search, not a courtroom drama and not a laboratory procedural. Continue on Daughter of the Scorpion.

The Wind’s Breathe is the second novel. Centuries after Birth of Wind, the cursed sword is found in Japan in 1996. Its new owner turns Wind’s samurai abilities against rivals in the construction industry. The published description is that specific: not a return to the fourteenth-century war, but the same binding put to work in a modern commercial fight. The title on the author’s materials uses this spelling, and so does this preview. Continue on The Wind’s Breathe.

The Vulture Will Lead You to Death follows terrorists who enter the United States from Cuba with a plan against the Port of Miami. They know a supertanker is docked there. They do not know that a ship carrying military supplies is stationed nearby, and that its presence could magnify the blast. The published description stops on that unawareness. The novel is the working out of an attack whose second weapon is the port itself. Continue on The Vulture Will Lead You to Death.
