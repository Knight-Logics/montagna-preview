THE NUCLEAR WEAPONS CONSPIRACY

SOURCE PAGE 17

This deserves its own section because it is one of the novel's strongest commercial hooks.

The manuscript develops a conspiracy involving:

Russian military technology

Iranian military interests

SS-N missile systems

Nuclear submarines

Nuclear warheads

Technology intended to enable Iran to develop its own nuclear capability

Evidence connecting Russian involvement to the operation

The manuscript later identifies six Russian-made Delta I nuclear submarines temporarily housed at Bander Beheshi.

SOURCE PAGE 24

DRAMATIC STAKES

SOURCE PAGE 25

The portfolio should clearly explain what happens if Walker fails.

The stakes aren't simply that soldiers may die.

The larger threat is that Iran could obtain the capability to develop and deploy nuclear weapons against the United States, while evidence of Russian assistance could have enormous international consequences.

That gives the story both a personal military stake and a global geopolitical stake.

SOURCE PAGE 32

SELECTED MANUSCRIPT EVIDENCE

SOURCE PAGE 33

This is particularly important for your submission package.

Rather than using generic quotations, I can select actual passages from the manuscript and identify their manuscript page locations.

For example, the manuscript contains important evidence around:

Pages 35–36: intelligence begins connecting Bander Beheshi with nuclear submarines and Russian missiles.

Page 294: evidence identifies Proushinsky and Marzuk and connects them to the six Russian-made Delta I submarines.

Pages 647–648: Operation Sandstorm and the naval deployment are established.

Pages 703–710: the assault begins and Walker's unit comes under attack.

Pages 758–759: Walker orders the recovery of files, computer disks and videotapes.

Around page 785: the surviving personnel complete their extraction.

That gives the publisher something concrete to examine rather than relying entirely on marketing language.

SOURCE PAGE 34

EDITORIAL CONSIDERATIONS

SOURCE PAGE 35

I would include a professional editorial note acknowledging that the manuscript contains:

Strong combat violence

Profanity

Sexual material

Ethnic/nationally charged dialogue

Military terminology

Graphic battlefield scenes

Rather than presenting these as flaws, the portfolio can identify them as editorial considerations for a future publishing edit, particularly if the novel is being prepared for a mainstream traditional publisher.
