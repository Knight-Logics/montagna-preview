DRAMATIC STAKES

SOURCE PAGE 25

The portfolio should clearly explain what happens if Walker fails.

The stakes aren't simply that soldiers may die.

The larger threat is that Iran could obtain the capability to develop and deploy nuclear weapons against the United States, while evidence of Russian assistance could have enormous international consequences.

That gives the story both a personal military stake and a global geopolitical stake.

This is particularly important for your submission package.

For example, the manuscript contains important evidence around:

Pages 35–36: intelligence begins connecting Bander Beheshi with nuclear submarines and Russian missiles.

Page 294: evidence identifies Proushinsky and Marzuk and connects them to the six Russian-made Delta I submarines.

Pages 647–648: Operation Sandstorm and the naval deployment are established.

That gives the publisher something concrete to examine rather than relying entirely on marketing language.

IN THE LIBRARY

Turning the pages of Sandstorm

The portfolio of Sandstorm is the set of pages in the reader above. This guide stays under the reader so the book can still be searched, shelved, and connected to the rest of the library.

Sandstorm sends American special operations soldiers into Iran. A rogue Russian admiral has sold Iran nuclear-tipped missiles launched from submarines. The team has to destroy the missiles and the submarines, and it has to bring back evidence of the transfer, evidence that could show how Iran acquired the means to build and launch a nuclear threat against the United States. The published description is a raid plus a proof.

Military thriller, espionage, and high stakes are the matching searches. Spy thriller fits the evidence as much as the explosives. Corporate corruption is a rising military-thriller phrase in the October 2026 lane snapshot. A rogue admiral selling missiles is a betrayal inside a navy, which is as close as this novel comes. It is not a story about a company. Trump’s Brass Escalator is the continuation: the evidence from Iran is what the president and vice-president mean to carry to the United Nations, before Trump Tower is attacked.

Read Sandstorm before that continuation. The Scorpion Trilogy is Walker’s other war, and it should not be folded into this mission unless you are only browsing the same kind of novel.

Sandstorm sits on the military and geopolitical shelf. Readers of that lane, across 1,737 trope mentions in the 28 weeks to 7 September 2026, keep asking for an action thriller, a page-turner, a spy thriller, a series continuation, an ensemble cast, high stakes, and espionage. Use those words because they describe the shelf. Do not add domestic thriller, a search that surged into February 2026, or romantasy, which is a different boom. “Mystery thriller books” held higher search interest than sci-fi novels or fantasy-trend queries from June 2025 through June 2026, with a peak in October 2025. This novel answers the thriller side of that interest. The Scorpion reading order, where Walker’s trilogy is the thread, is on the Scorpion series page. Five further novels sit one link away from this page. Each note below is the published situation of that book, not a new plot. Follow the one that matches the search you actually typed.

Eagle’s Talon is the opening novel of the Scorpion Trilogy. In the published story, Saddam Hussein sends a terrorist group to assassinate the American president in an effort to end sanctions. Robert Walker and his Special Forces troops are sent to stop the attack, and the mission then takes them into Iraq to capture the terrorist leader and bring him to the United States for trial. Continue on Eagle’s Talon.

Night Wind closes the trilogy’s published arc on this site. The sword is discovered by the FBI. The agent who takes responsibility brings it to the United States and learns it is not an ordinary weapon. Wind remains bound to protect the person who possesses it. That duty, not a new origin story, is what the third novel puts in a new country. Continue on Night Wind.

The Vector is the preview’s plain example of a geopolitical thriller. Iran answers a war with a covert biological attack on the United States. Eight volunteers are fitted with false teeth that hold a deadly agent and are sent through Mexico toward eight American cities. Their orders are to release the agent and spread the infection before it kills them. The weapon in the published description is a person, not a missile. Continue on The Vector.

God’s Sword of Justice places terrorists in the control room of the Indian Point nuclear power plant in New York. They shut off cooling water to the reactor. The published description gives the region a nine-hour window before a catastrophic meltdown and the contamination that would follow. Walker’s elite soldiers have to stop the attackers before the plant itself becomes the weapon. Continue on God’s Sword of Justice.

Bio-Level Three sends specialized Dragon Fire troops into the Libyan desert. A rebel-led military command is secretly building twin chemical and biological factories. American intelligence finds the project. The president orders the factories destroyed before they can operate. The published description is a preemption story: stop the buildings before the weapons exist, not investigate a crime after a city is already sick. Continue on Bio-Level Three.
