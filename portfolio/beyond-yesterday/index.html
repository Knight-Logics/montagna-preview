THE STORY AT

A GLANCE

SOURCE PAGE 05

What if humanity survived the end of the world—but discovered that survival itself was no longer enough?

In BEYOND YESTERDAY, a devastating nuclear war transforms Earth into a dying world. More than ninety percent of the planet's population is ultimately lost, while radiation, environmental destruction, and the long-term consequences of the catastrophe make the surface increasingly hostile to human life. The manuscript establishes that the survivors have spent almost thirty years underground, watching their numbers steadily decline.

By 2054, the survivors face an even more terrifying problem: they can no longer reproduce.

THE CENTRAL PREMISE

SOURCE PAGE 08

The survivors cannot simply travel backward and live in the past.

Their bodies have changed after decades of exposure to radiation and the altered environment of the postwar world. Dr. Dewitt explains that returning permanently to the past would not solve their problem. Instead, they must find a way to bring elements of the past into the future.

The first plan is biological:

Travel into earlier periods.

Obtain reproductive material.

Return it to the future.

Attempt to use it to restore human reproduction.

But the story gradually expands this objective.

The future survivors begin physically retrieving human subjects from the past.

Their mission is no longer simply to recover biological material.

They are attempting to rebuild humanity itself.

THE

TIME-TRAVEL MISSION

SOURCE PAGE 16

As the story progresses, the future survivors become increasingly organized in their missions.

The time-travel crews are composed of trained personnel supported by military security teams. Their assignments involve entering carefully selected historical periods, locating suitable human subjects, securing them, conducting medical procedures, and returning them to the future.

One mission successfully retrieves five male pilots from five aircraft. The operation itself produces another powerful storm as the craft returns through the temporal vortex.

Later missions retrieve additional people from the past.

In one operation, eight women are brought back to the underground facility for medical examination and processing. The survivors are attempting to assemble enough people from the past to establish a viable population for humanity's future.

PROFESSIONAL SYNOPSIS

SOURCE PAGE 32

BEYOND YESTERDAY opens in 2025, as escalating conflicts between the world's nuclear powers develop into a catastrophic global nuclear exchange. The attacks spread rapidly across continents, destroying cities, military installations and entire regions. The resulting radioactive contamination and environmental devastation transform the planet.

The survivors who remain retreat into heavily protected underground facilities.

Nearly three decades later, the surviving population has been reduced to fewer than one thousand people in one major underground community. Commander Andy Graff calls an emergency meeting when Dr. Richard Dewitt delivers devastating news: the survivors are no longer capable of reproducing. Their women can no longer become pregnant, and the surviving men no longer possess viable sperm.

The human race has survived the nuclear war only to discover that it may now be biologically incapable of continuing.

Dewitt reveals that the government had secretly preserved technology capable of time travel. The survivors initially plan to use the technology to retrieve sperm and eggs from earlier generations and transport them into the future.

SOURCE PAGE 33

But the experiments lead to a much more radical strategy.

The survivors begin sending specialized crews into the past to retrieve living human subjects.

Their missions take them into historical periods where they can secretly locate people suitable for their population program.

The operations eventually intersect with one of history's most famous unexplained disappearances: Flight 19.

The five aircraft that disappeared in 1945 become targets of the future survivors' operation. The time-travel craft emerges in the Bermuda Triangle, where the temporal vortex coincides with the sudden development of severe weather.

The survivors discover that time travel itself carries a cost.

The vortex produces storms in the past.

Every mission potentially creates another hurricane.

Every return leaves evidence.

WHY THE TITLE MATTERS

SOURCE PAGE 36

Yesterday = the people and history that existed before the catastrophe.

Today = the underground survivors struggling to remain alive.

Tomorrow = a future that may depend upon stealing from yesterday.

AUTHOR

SOURCE PAGE 40

Stephen Francis Montagna is the author of BEYOND YESTERDAY, a large-scale speculative thriller exploring humanity's struggle to survive after global nuclear devastation.

The manuscript is structured around an ambitious premise: a future civilization attempts to use time travel not to alter history, but to obtain the human population it needs to continue existing.
